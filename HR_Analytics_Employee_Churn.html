HR Analytics: Employee Churn Prediction¶

Project Objective¶

The objective of this project is to use employee data and machine learning to predict whether an employee is likely to leave the organization. The project demonstrates how machine learning can be integrated into HR operations to support proactive employee retention and workforce planning.

1. Business Problem¶

Employee turnover can create significant challenges for organizations, including recruitment costs, training costs, loss of organizational knowledge, and workforce disruption.

From an HR perspective, identifying employees who may be at a higher risk of leaving can help organizations take proactive retention measures.

In this project, employee data is analyzed and a machine learning classification model is developed to predict employee churn.

Key HR Question¶

Can employee characteristics such as satisfaction, performance evaluation, number of projects, working hours, tenure, salary, department, and promotion history be used to predict employee churn?

2. Dataset Introduction¶

The dataset contains employee-level information related to job satisfaction, performance evaluation, workload, tenure, work accidents, promotions, department, salary, and employee churn.

The target variable is churn, which indicates whether an employee left the organization.

The remaining variables are used as potential predictors of employee churn.

8. Train-Test Split¶

The dataset is divided into training and testing sets.

The training data is used to develop the machine learning model, while the testing data is used to evaluate how well the model performs on previously unseen employee records.

A test size of 25% is used, meaning 75% of the observations are used for training and 25% for testing.

In [38]:

    features,
    target,

    random_state=42,

)

9. Decision Tree Classification¶

A Decision Tree Classifier is used to predict employee churn.

The model works by creating a series of decision rules based on employee characteristics and dividing employees into groups with similar churn outcomes.The Gini impurity criterion is used to determine the quality of each split.

13. HR Interpretation¶

The machine learning model provides HR with a data-driven approach to identifying patterns associated with employee churn.

The results can potentially support:

  • Proactive employee retention initiatives
  • Workforce planning
  • Identification of areas requiring employee engagement interventions
  • Review of workload and working-hour patterns
  • Evaluation of compensation and promotion practices

The model should be considered a decision-support tool rather than a replacement for HR judgement.

14. HR Recommendations¶

Based on the model results, HR can consider using employee churn predictions as an early-warning mechanism.

Potential applications include:

  1. Identifying employee groups that may require greater retention attention.
  2. Reviewing employee satisfaction and workload patterns.
  3. Strengthening employee engagement initiatives.
  4. Evaluating opportunities for career development and promotion.
  5. Using data-driven insights to support workforce planning.

Any intervention should be supported by appropriate HR judgement and additional employee-level context rather than relying solely on the model.

15. Conclusion¶

This project demonstrates how machine learning can be applied to an HR problem such as employee churn prediction.

A Decision Tree classification model was developed using employee characteristics to predict whether an employee is likely to leave the organization.

The project demonstrates the potential of integrating data analytics and machine learning into HR processes to support proactive retention, workforce planning, and evidence-based decision-making.
